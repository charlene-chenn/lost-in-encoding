Abstract

Tactile learning pipelines often inherit their representation format from the sensing hardware, without establishing which physical properties each format preserves. We make this choice measurable with a magnetic tri-axial array, whose readings can be rendered as either a tactile image or a point cloud from the same contact. Using 180 synchronised tactile-proprioceptive recordings on a uSkin LEAP hand, spanning five stiffness classes, six textures, and six exploratory procedures on a fixed cube, we measure property retention in each encoding. Evaluation is conducted with four classical classifiers with differing inductive biases and a matched deep architecture per format. Across our experiments, point clouds discriminate stiffness more reliably, while tactile images discriminate texture better. Both results hold across the classical classifiers, pointing to the encoding rather than the decision rule. Thus, a shared tactile input supports different properties depending on how they are spatially indexed. Handcrafted features are 32.22% better in stiffness and 1.11% better in texture discrimination compared to learned encoders.

Related Links

Representing Data in Robotic Tactile Perception reviews tactile data representations, such as images and point clouds, and how each is paired with processing suited to what it retains.

Tactile encoders are typically pretrained on large interaction datasets and evaluated on downstream tasks, as in UniT, T3, Sparsh-skin, and SITR. AnyTouch ranks encoders on shared benchmarks with linear probing.

Other work evaluates tactile representations through policy success, as in Making Sense of Vision and Touch, or through planning outcomes in vision-tactile world models, as in ContactWorld.

TacGen probes physical properties to evaluate touch as a modality, rather than to compare tactile encoding formats.

Capturing Visual Environment Structure Correlates with Control Performance probes what visual representations retain against ground-truth environment state, an analysis that has yet to be applied to tactile encoding formats.
